What TrustOS reads

Trust isn’t an opinion about you. It’s the state of a relationship with you.

Reputation measures what people say about you — aggregate, outside-in, and always after the fact. Trust is held by specific stakeholders and earned through experience — so it moves before the results do. That’s the difference between a rear-view mirror and an instrument.

The same event. Two very different instruments.

Reputation · what’s said Trust · what’s believed

Illustrative pattern

Grounded in peer-reviewed research: shifts in employee sentiment predict next-quarter earnings surprises (J. Financial Economics, 2019); employee-voice language predicts misconduct before it becomes public (Management Science, 2022); markets take years to fully price employee trust (J. Financial Economics, 2011).

Reputation is what people say about what you’ve done. Trust is whether they believe in what you do next.

TrustOS reads it relationship by relationship — self-awareness, made measurable — so you act in the warning window, not after it.

The pattern across every sector

Look at the short edge. It’s nearly always Connection.

In eleven of the twelve sectors we’ve read, Connection is the weakest layer — consistently behind what organisations say about themselves. It’s the say-do gap no single function owns: where trust leaks, and the first thing a TrustOS read makes visible. In plain terms: organisations are far better at talking than at knowing how they land — and it shows up in the relationship before it shows up in the results.

That gap has a name. It’s the alignment problem — the distance between what an organisation believes and says, and what its stakeholders actually experience. Measuring it is what TrustOS does. Closing it is what MissionCTRL does.
