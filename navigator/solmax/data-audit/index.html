What Solmax already knows

Before we ask anyone at Solmax a single new question, we are working out what the business already holds. This page asks you what exists — not for the files, and not for anything you would have to go and build.

You will see only the lines that touch your work. “We don’t have that”, “not my area” and “I don’t know” are real answers here, and each takes one click.

What TrustOS is

Solmax measures almost everything it does — and almost nothing about how it is experienced. TrustOS fixes that. It asks every group Solmax depends on the same questions, then turns the answers into a single 0–100 score you can track, so you can see where trust is strong and where it is leaking before the numbers move.

Reputation is what people say about what you’ve done. Trust is whether they believe in what you do next.

ClarityWhat’s said — the promises, the messages, the published word.
ConnectionWhat stakeholders actually experience when they deal with Solmax.
ConfidenceWhat can be proved — the evidence behind the claims.

Step five of six

Where it comes from

Step four asked whether a document exists and who holds it. This step asks something the audit has never asked: where a signal is collected today — and where it is not, where it could be. Seventeen sources, one dropdown each. “No” and “I don’t know” are one click and move you straight on.

First, the honest one — asked once

If the board asked you tomorrow to prove that Solmax is trusted by the people it depends on, what could you actually put in front of them? Pick the strongest thing that is true today, not the thing you wish were true.

No wrong answer. This is the one line in the whole return that is a judgement rather than a fact, and it is here to sanity-check the inventory below: if the list says the data exists and this says nothing could be shown, one of the two is wrong and we would rather know now.

The seventeen sources

The first seven matter most. They are about what people actually experience — surveys, complaints, quality and safety records — and they decide the readiness tier below and how much new survey work would have to be paid for.

What that adds up to

The Navigator worked out Solmax’s data readiness from tick-boxes, before any of this was scoped or priced. This is the same calculation on real answers: the share of the seven experience sources collected routinely, cut at the same two boundaries, so the two numbers can be put side by side.

This is a data-readiness tier. It is not a trust score, and not a trust band. It says how much of the evidence already exists — nothing at all about how much Solmax is trusted.
