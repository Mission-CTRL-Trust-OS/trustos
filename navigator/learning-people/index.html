Three moves a Snapshot would validate. Each one, proven elsewhere.

This read shows the shape. A Snapshot tells you which of these your stakeholders would actually reward — before you commit.
01

Make the outcomes claim the most verifiable in the sector

A Snapshot validates: how learners, employers and partners actually experience the outcome promise — so the number that wins enrolments becomes published, methodology-backed evidence that survives the “is it legit?” search every prospective career-changer runs.
CIRR — tech-education schools built a shared standard for independently verified job-outcome reporting, turning a sector-wide credibility problem into a competitive advantage for the schools that publish.
See the case
The Council on Integrity in Results Reporting (CIRR) was created by career-tech schools to standardise how graduation rates, job placement and salaries are reported — 100% of student outcomes, independently checked, comparable across providers. In a market where outcome claims are the product, the schools that publish to a verifiable standard convert scepticism into enrolments; the claim itself becomes the moat. CIRR — standardised outcomes reporting
02

Give the new chapter a stated intent — and a measured baseline

A Snapshot validates: what each stakeholder group believes Learning People stands for today — so the new ownership can state its intent against a measured starting point, and every improvement from here is attributable, provable and yours to claim.
Microsoft — Satya Nadella opened his tenure by naming culture as the variable to change, saying so publicly, and measuring the change relentlessly — the trust rebuild preceded the growth story.
See the case
Nadella’s transformation of Microsoft — documented in Hit Refresh — began with a new leader stating intent plainly and treating culture and trust as measurable operating variables rather than atmosphere. Because the intent was on the record early and progress was tracked against it, every subsequent improvement compounded the leadership story instead of disappearing into it. The same window is open in a company’s first year under new ownership — and only in the first year. Microsoft — Hit Refresh
03

Fix the named moments where the learner echo frays

A Snapshot validates: which specific moments — the sales call, the finance agreement, the cancellation policy, mid-course support — actually drive detraction, and which fixes each stakeholder group would reward first, so investment lands where trust compounds fastest.
Octopus Energy built category-leading trust in a low-trust sector by obsessing over the exact moments that generate complaints — and converted service reputation into market share.
See the case
Octopus Energy entered a sector defined by mistrust — billing disputes, call queues, switching pain — and treated each named irritant as an engineering problem, publishing its service record where every prospect could check it. The result is one of the UK’s most-recommended consumer brands and sustained share growth through a period when the sector’s trust collapsed. The lesson for any provider whose reviews split loud-positive against a sharp negative tail: the tail is a list of fixable moments, not a brand problem. Octopus Energy — public service record
