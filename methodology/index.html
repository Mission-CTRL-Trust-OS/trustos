How we score.
The model, in brief.

What TrustOS measures, why it moves before the numbers do, and what turns an estimate into evidence. The full working, we share in a conversation.

Trust is usually assumed and rarely measured. TrustOS measures it — as one number — the Trust Index — a leadership team can act on, with the working shown.

What is trust measurement?

Trust measurement is the practice of quantifying how far an organisation’s stakeholders — its customers, employees, investors, regulators and communities — actually trust it, tracked over time as a leading indicator of reputation and business performance. Most organisations manage trust on instinct. TrustOS turns it into one number — the Trust Index — a leadership team can act on, built the same way in every sector, with the working shown.

What is a trust signal?

A trust signal is a measured, repeatable read of that trust — not a sentiment headline or a brand-awareness score, but the Trust Index, a 0–100 composite built from three drivers — Clarity (what’s said), Connection (what stakeholders actually experience) and Confidence (what can be proved) — on a foundation of Integrity. It moves before the performance numbers do, which is what makes it a leading indicator of resilience and growth rather than a lagging one.

The shape: three drivers on a foundation

Suggested focus

TrustOS reads trust as alignment — between what an organisation intends, what its stakeholders expect, and what they actually experience. Three drivers — Clarity, Connection and Confidence — sit on a foundation of Integrity, and roll up into one 0–100 Trust Index, with the full breakdown always shown beneath it, never instead of it. The triangle deforms to the scores: a strong, balanced organisation fills the shape; a lopsided one pulls a corner in, showing exactly where trust is thinning.

Bands: what we publish, and what we don’t

The Trust Index runs 0–100, but a score is only ever published for a read we have actually measured. Sector POVs, Navigators and the Explorer are built from public signal, which is limited by nature — precise enough to show a shape and a weak edge, not precise enough to carry a number. So those are published as bands.

ExposedTrust does no work for you. Claims are discounted on arrival and every relationship is re-argued from scratch.
FragileTrust exists but is not load-bearing. It holds in good conditions and gives way under pressure or change.
FunctionalTrust is real, but uneven. Some relationships carry the business; others are quietly costing it — and the difference only shows under change.
ResilientTrust absorbs shocks. You get the benefit of the doubt, recover faster, and claims land first time.
DefiningTrust is an asset others measure themselves against. You set the terms the category follows.

Inside an installed read, a client sees both: the Index and its three drivers, scored per stakeholder group, with direction of travel and what would move each one. That is the difference between an estimate and evidence — and it is why the numbers live in the read, not on this website.

From estimate to evidence: Sense → Measure → Act

A Navigator or sector POV is read from public signal alone — annual reports, disclosure, news, the public footprint. That makes it a directional estimate, not a statistically validated measurement, and we label it that way on purpose. A Snapshot replaces the estimate with primary research across your stakeholders — measured against a bar that keeps rising. The honesty is the point.
